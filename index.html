Warning Signs

How to Know Your Account Is Compromised

Watch for these red flags — the sooner you spot them, the less damage an attacker can do.

🚨
Act immediately if you notice any of these signs.

Even a single sign can indicate a breach. Do not wait to confirm multiple signs before taking action.

📧

Unexpected password reset emails

You receive password reset or verification codes you didn't request — someone is actively trying to access your account.

🌍

Logins from unknown locations

Your account's activity log shows sign-ins from cities, countries or devices you've never used.

📤

Messages sent without your knowledge

Friends report receiving strange messages or links from your account that you never sent.

🔑

Password no longer works

You're locked out of your own account — the attacker has already changed your credentials.

⚙️

Changed account settings

Your profile, recovery email, phone number, or security settings were modified without your knowledge.

💳

Unauthorized transactions

Unexpected purchases, subscriptions, or fund transfers appear in your linked payment accounts.

🤖

Spam sent to your contacts

Your email or social media contacts receive phishing links or suspicious content from your account.

🛡️

Security alerts from the platform

The service itself notifies you of a sign-in attempt, a new device added, or a security policy change.

Immediate Actions

What To Do Right Now

Follow these steps in order. Speed is critical — complete the urgent steps before anything else.

1

Change your password immediately Urgent

Use a strong, unique password you've never used anywhere else — at least 16 characters mixing letters, numbers and symbols. If you're locked out, use the platform's account recovery flow right away.

2

Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) Urgent

Turn on 2FA before changing anything else if possible. Use an authenticator app (e.g. Google Authenticator, Authy, or a hardware key like YubiKey) rather than SMS, which can be intercepted.

3

Sign out of all active sessions Urgent

Most platforms offer a "Sign out of all devices" option in security settings. Use it to immediately revoke the attacker's active session.

4

Check & update recovery options High priority

Verify that your recovery email address and phone number belong to you and haven't been replaced by the attacker. Remove any suspicious options you don't recognise.

5

Revoke third-party app permissions High priority

Go to your account's connected apps or OAuth permissions page and remove any application you don't recognise or no longer use. Attackers often maintain access through trusted third-party apps.

6

Change passwords on linked accounts High priority

If you reused the same password elsewhere, change it on every affected service. Attackers perform credential stuffing — automatically testing leaked credentials on hundreds of sites.

7

Review recent account activity Important

Check login history, sent messages, file access logs, and any other audit trail the platform provides. Document suspicious activity — you may need this for a report.

8

Alert your contacts Important

Notify your friends, family and colleagues that your account was compromised. Ask them to ignore and delete any suspicious messages they may have received from you.

9

Report the breach to the platform Follow-up

Use the platform's official security or abuse reporting channel. This helps them protect other users and can assist with recovering your account if other methods fail.

10

Consider reporting to authorities Follow-up

If financial data was stolen or you're being extorted, file a report with your national cybercrime unit (e.g. IC3 in the US, Cybermalveillance.gouv.fr in France, or Action Fraud in the UK).

Prevention

Protect Yourself Going Forward

Once you've recovered your account, adopt these habits to keep attackers out for good.

🔑

Use a Password Manager

Generate and store a unique, strong password for every single account. Recommended tools: Bitwarden (free & open-source), 1Password, or KeePassXC.

📱

Enable 2FA Everywhere

Use an authenticator app or hardware security key for every account that supports it. Avoid SMS-based 2FA where possible — opt for TOTP or FIDO2.

🎣

Recognise Phishing

Always verify the sender's domain. Hover over links before clicking. Legitimate companies never ask for your password via email or chat.

🔄

Keep Software Updated

Apply security patches to your OS, browser, and apps promptly. Enable automatic updates to close vulnerabilities before attackers can exploit them.

🌐

Be Cautious on Public Wi-Fi

Avoid logging into sensitive accounts on public networks. Use a trusted VPN if you must connect from a café, airport, or hotel.

🔍

Monitor for Data Breaches

Sign up for breach notifications at HaveIBeenPwned. Change your password immediately if your email appears in a breach.

📧

Use an Alias or Separate Email

Services like SimpleLogin or Apple Hide My Email let you create disposable aliases, limiting exposure and making it easy to spot which service leaked your data.

🛡️

Review App Permissions Regularly

Periodically check which third-party apps have access to your accounts. Remove anything you no longer use — every permission is a potential attack surface.
